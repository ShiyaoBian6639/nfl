## Bottom line

1. **This is a writing and framing contest, not a modeling contest.** 70% of the rubric is non-modeling (Football 30%, Writeup 20%, Viz 20%), there is no leaderboard in 2027, and the only first-hand winner's guide that exists is seven tips about *audience*, not methods ([Chang](https://medium.com/@mpchang17/my-team-won-the-2024-big-data-bowl-ca9f668d011d)). Budget ≥1/3 of Oct 10 → Jan 6 for prose and figures, and **write the 110-word lede before the model is finished** — if we can't fill "trait / effect+CI / decision changed" with specifics, the scope is still too wide.
2. **The single claim that decides our Football Score is "it beats draft slot."** The peer-reviewed base rate is that combine *measurables* explain ~2.6% of performance variance; the public state of the art for a rookie prior is literally `9.872 − 4.619·ln(draft_number)` ([nfeloqb QB.py](https://github.com/greerreNFL/nfeloqb/blob/main/nfeloqb/DataModels/QB.py)). Every judge is a team analyst who already believes the combine is near-useless. The lede must be *"controlling for `draft_overall_pick`, position, and exposure, this 10 Hz trait still moves outcome Y by Z (95% CI …), and neither `forty`, `three_cone`, nor `ngs_final_score` captures it."*
3. **The only defensible novelty is shape-of-motion inside the drills — not speed.** Tracked peak speed is essentially a restatement of the official 40 (r ≈ −0.98 in a public audit), tracking-derived 40 times run ~0.3 s slow, and `dis` under-counts (~37 yd over a 40-yd dash). The white space is braking impulse, turn radius/bend, transition cost, left/right asymmetry, speed-retention-through-catch, cue latency — things a stopwatch physically cannot express, and which NGS's own **monotonically-constrained** draft model structurally cannot represent (a monotone model cannot encode an *optimum*).
4. **Scope must be one position group × one drill family × one per-opportunity outcome.** Eight of eight past winners did exactly that; the documented 2025 finalist worked n = 3,029 plays. The host's own words: *"Pick something small -- a position, a play type, a scheme -- and try and do it well."* With ~100–120 players per position group (and 40–70 after snap filtering), a battery across five groups is guaranteed noise.
5. **Figure 4 is the whole competition.** No prior Big Data Bowl could produce *the same athlete's combine drill trace beside his NFL in-game trace at the same 10 Hz, same units, same code path*. Build that pairing early and design the narrative to earn it. 2023–2025 is the **complete history** of NFL combine sensor tracking — there is no fourth year and no corpus anyone could have mined, so every finding here is first-generation.

**The eight-year winning formula** ([canon](https://operations.nfl.com/programs-initiatives/innovation/big-data-bowl)):
- **One** position group × **one** decision moment × **one** binary/continuous outcome.
- **Define a new countable football event** that currently has no stat. The 2024 winner invented the *Missed Tackle Opportunity* (tackle prob >75% for ≥0.5 s, then drops, nobody tackles within 1 s) and surfaced ~4× more than the NFL's charted missed tackles — "a whole class of defensive error that previously had no stat."
- **The recurrent device is the counterfactual**: expected-vs-actual. 2020 expected rushing yards, 2022 optimal punt-return path, 2021/2026 ghost defenders, 2026 winner's SLIME. *Our translation:* an expected-trajectory model from the drill tracking (expected x/y/s path given height/weight/split), scoring each prospect as observed-minus-expected.
- **Simple models.** 2024 winners tested neural nets and chose XGBoost because it "generalized better"; a 2024 runner-up used **pure physics, no ML**, and was later published in *Scientific Reports*. With n=510 a transformer is a liability.
- **A sayable name.** SLIME, STRAIN, COYOTE, CPP/OPLE/DPLE, Fractional Tackles, Safety Entropy. Named after the **football concept, never the model**. Titles are idioms/puns: *"Ghostbusters: Back off man, I'm a Data Scientist!"*, *"Between the Lines: How Do We Measure Pressure?"*, *"Under Cover 2"*, *"No Edge, No Chance"*.
- **Terminate in a unit a football person budgets in**: plays, snaps, yards, points. Safety Entropy's headline: *"our model gives the offense ≈27 more 'free' plays per season."*

**Andrew Patton (NFL Director of Analytical Research & Governance), after grading a cycle** ([thread](https://x.com/anpatt7/status/1746209633562443948)): *"Make sure you answer the prompt. ## Prior art & evidence base

**The combine-predicts-NFL literature is largely negative — this is our motivation, not our obstacle.**
- Combine measures explain on the order of **~2.6%** of performance variance; no predictive ability for rookie-year performance, weak correlation with 5-year performance; only broad jump and 20-yd shuttle weakly associated with draft placement ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/the-predictive-ability-of-the-physical-skills-used-at-the-nfl-combine-to-predict-draft-status/)).
- Robbins (JSCR, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001202)): three-cone **not significant** for RB performance (p = 0.650).
- [arXiv 2303.05774](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05774): the 3-cone had the **least** feature importance despite being the drill perceived as most important for draft position.
- SumerSports' public position-by-position treatment of how little the 40 predicts: [link](https://sumersports.com/the-zone/nfl-combine-by-position-how-do-the-40-yard-dash-and-other-drills-predict-nfl-success/).
- Gil Brandt / NFL.com's own framing: combine data is a **tiebreaker** between two evenly-graded prospects, not a primary tool ([link](https://www.nfl.com/news/what-does-the-combine-data-mean-depends-on-your-position-09000d5d80ecd9a3)).
- **Clark et al.** (JSCR, [PubMed 28658072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28658072/)): at the combine, max velocity correlates r = 0.72/0.83/0.94 with 9.1/18.3/36.6 m times, but the acceleration time-constant **τ was identical** between fast and slow groups (0.768 ± 0.068 vs 0.773 ± 0.070 s) *when derived from split times*. With 10 Hz trajectories we can fit τ and v_max **per attempt**. If the published finding is that τ doesn't separate at the combine, showing that τ *does* predict an in-game behavior is exactly the non-obvious linkage the theme asks for.

**The incumbent we are competing against is in our own feature table.** The NGS Draft Model ([NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-draft-model-can-predict-prospects-pro-success-0ap3000001103347)) is position-specific XGBoost across 12 positions with **monotonic constraints**, trained on combine results since 2003 + college stats since 2005, predicting *"the likelihood that the player will become an NFL starter or Pro Bowler within the first three seasons,"* output on a 50–99 scale. Validation anecdote: all 13 of the 2019 class with final score ≥92 started ≥4 games as rookies. Per [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/beyond-the-stopwatch-inside-the-nfls-new-interactive-combine-dashboard), success prediction *"relies on threshold-based interactions rather than linear relationships."* **Two consequences: (a) `ngs_final_score`/`ngs_athleticism_score`/`ngs_college_production_score` are LEAKAGE if used as features against `player_career_successes.csv` — their training target is nearly our outcome; they are the BASELINE to beat. (b) Monotonicity is a structural weakness: a monotone model cannot represent an optimum, so any tracking feature with a non-monotone relationship to success (an *ideal*, not maximal, deceleration rate) is an edge we can name explicitly.**

**Teams already do this manually — formalizing it IS the prompt.** Per Zebra Sports' VP ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/draft/news/2024-nfl-draft-how-teams-use-data-to-build-their-boards-from-athletic-evaluation-to-cognitive-assessment/)): teams look at in-game tracking for their best players, cross-reference combine stats that correlate with production, and **reverse-engineer the combine numbers to learn how to scout for the trait**. Use the phrase *"scout for the trait."* Mike Lopez confirmed this is the first BDB centred on combine data, scoped to WR/TE/OL/DL/DB, framed against the "underwear Olympics" dismissal ([Yahoo/AP](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nfl-data-competition-focus-matters-123000120.html)). NGS's Mike Band: *"You don't have to be the fastest player, but you got to be fast enough"* — a direct invitation to test **threshold/step-function** effects, not linear ones.

**Public BDB-2027 work already published — including two negative results we must not repeat.**
- [Hermes-25/nfl-bdb-2027-mirror-recovery](https://github.com/Hermes-25/nfl-bdb-2027-mirror-recovery): PASS_PRO_MIRROR reversal features (normalized velocity shortfall, incoming lateral speed, lateral excursion) **added to** conventional combine measures made rookie-OL pressure prediction **worse** (dev-mean Brier 0.084587 → 0.083513 the wrong way, Δ = −0.0009). Only 57 of 121 OL eligible; one attempt per lineman → no test-retest.
- [faith-amanze/combine-tracking-audit](https://github.com/faith-amanze/combine-tracking-audit): weight + position alone explain **adj. R² = 0.464** of DL `player_get_off` (n=68); reliable pass-rush *speed-profile* drill features showed **no** link to weight-adjusted get-off (n=65, r between −0.03 and +0.02; F-test p = 0.61 vs the official 40). Also: tracked peak speed ≈ official 40 (r ≈ −0.98); tracked 40 times ~0.3 s slow (r = 0.91 with official); `dis` sums to ~37 yd over a 40-yd dash.
- [Kenjaro70 PR #3](https://github.com/Kenjaro70/NFL-Big-Data-Bowl-2027-KR/pull/3): a cut detector validated on SHORT_SHUTTLE — **94% of shuttle reps show exactly two ≥150° reversals**, i.e. the plants are reliably machine-detectable. Reported split-half reliabilities for ~60 WRs: speed into break **0.58**, speed retention **0.51**, peak turning acceleration **0.53**. 4 of 13 features were too noisy to use.
- [bansal1600/kaggle-NFL-big-data-2027](https://github.com/bansal1600/kaggle-NFL-big-data-2027): EDA — position counts **WR 107, TE 42, DB 122, OL 121, DL 118**; 463k combine-tracking rows; ~55 position-specific drills beyond the 40/3-cone/shuttle.

**The presentation conventions of metrics front offices actually adopt** — five shared traits: a named, football-legible unit; an explicit "beats the obvious baseline" test; **published year-over-year reliability with an outside-sport benchmark**; honest limitation sections; and the proprietary number shown *next to* familiar stats rather than replacing them.
- **nfelo**: the measurement layer stays deliberately dumb and auditable (538's linear VALUE formula, published to the coefficient: `−2.2·PassAtt + 3.7·Comp + PassYds/5 + 11.3·PassTD − 14.1·INT − 8·Sacks − 1.1·RushAtt + 0.6·RushYds + 15.9·RushTD`) while all sophistication lives in the updating layer. Hard exchange rates: **25 Elo = 1 point of margin; 1 VALUE = 3.3 Elo** (so ~7.6 VALUE ≈ 1 point). Headline column is **"Value v Avg Starter → Points"** with **Δ Week / Δ Year** columns; Elo is demoted into a "Ratings" group next to Passer Rtg and QBR. And it argues against itself in public: *"there is no perfect metric for measuring QB play, as shown by our analysis."* ([qb-rankings](https://www.nfeloapp.com/qb-rankings/), [metric study](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/what-are-the-best-metrics-for-nfl-quarterbacks/), [repo](https://github.com/greerreNFL/nfeloqb))
- **ESPN win rates** — our validation blueprint ([run block/stop](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/29813062/introducing-new-nfl-run-blocking-run-stopping-stats-how-run-block-win-rate-run-stop-win-rate-work), [2025 rebuild](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/49672562/nfl-new-pass-block-push-rush-win-rates-formula-takeaways-analytics)): a **monotone outcome ladder** (average rushing gain falls 7.2 yd at 0 run-stop wins → −0.7 yd at 7 wins); a **leave-one-criterion-out** robustness check (*"removing the 3-yard tackle criterion produces identical patterns"*); **per-position** YoY reliability (OT 0.71, G 0.51, edge 0.67, LB 0.48–0.72) — never pooled; a single named interpretable threshold (**2.5 s**); marginal effects in percentage points of a real outcome ("chance of a sack increases by eight percentage points"); and a **benchmark against another sport**: *"MLB batting averages have between 0.4 to 0.5 year-to-year reliability."* Limitations stated in the body: *"the system knows neither the blocker nor defender assignments."*
- **ESPN Receiver Tracking Metrics** ([link](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/34649390/espn-receiver-tracking-metrics-how-new-nfl-stats-work-open-catch-yac-scores)): 0–99 scale, 50 = average, decomposed into Open/Catch/YAC with published reliabilities (0.61 / 0.38 / 0.35 / overall 0.52) and an external-validity table (PFF grade 0.76, YPRR 0.76, PFR AV 0.68, Madden 0.59). Critically: *"the three components generally do not correlate with each other… our metrics are truly isolating three independent skills."* **Copy this component-independence check — it pre-empts "isn't this just the 40?", the most likely judge objection.**
- **Honest weak numbers are a credibility convention**: Tej Seth's RYOE explains ~22% of rushing-yard variance with YoY r = **0.21**, stated plainly ([repo](https://github.com/tejseth/RYOE)). PFF: coverage grades explain and predict better than pass rush *"at the expense of year-to-year stability"* (team pass-rush→coverage grade r = 0.17) ([link](https://www.pff.com/news/pro-pff-data-study-coverage-vs-pass-rush)). **Sabin's warning**: on a thin slice (QB, 3rd-and-≤5, first halves, 2011–23) EPA's YoY stability is **0.002 vs 0.093 for plain yards** — a ~47× gap ([link](https://sabinanalytics.com/blog/2024/01:05:slow-down-using-epa-for-everything/)). *Do not slice thin.*
- **SumerSports** bakes stability into the number (*"how rare a player's seasonal performance is and how much we can trust it will continue to be rare"*) then buries it in depth-chart language: **Blue-Chip (10/9/8) / Starter (7/6) / Sub-Starter (5/4)** ([glossary](https://help.sumersports.com/en/article/terminology-glossary)). **Tier labels beat numbers for the Football Score — that's what gets quoted in a draft room.**
- **Unit hierarchy of credibility for this audience: yards > points > wins > percentile > z-score.** DVOA = % above/below average; DYAR = value above *replacement* translated into yardage; PFF WAR = wins with replacement pinned to "a 3-13 team." DVOA/DYAR exist as a *pair* because teams want both per-play efficiency and volume-weighted total.

**NGS's own operational definitions — use their vocabulary, it's free Football Score** ([pressure probability](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-introduction-to-pressure-probability), [2025 metrics](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-new-advanced-metrics-you-need-to-know-for-the-2025-nfl-season), [glossary](https://nextgenstats.nfl.com/glossary)): pressure = pressure probability **>75%**; league-average time to pressure **2.9 s**; quick pressure **<2.5 s**; league pass-rush pressure rate **10.3%**; positive rush rate **54%**; cushion = WR-to-defender distance at snap; separation = distance to nearest defender at catch/incompletion; get-off = average seconds for a defender to cross the LOS after the snap (Micah Parsons 0.33 s). The 2025 NGS "coverage responsibility" transformer assigns the defender *responsible* for the targeted receiver (not nearest) — almost certainly what populates `coverage_assignment` / `coverage_assignment_at_snap`.

**Drill mechanics — the football facts that make features legitimate.**
- **FORTY_YARD_DASH**: self-started, hand-start / laser-finish → **contains zero reaction time**. Never claim it predicts get-off; a judge will spot it instantly. 0–10 yd = drive/horizontal-force phase; 20–40 = "flying 20" max-velocity mechanics. Fit v(t) = v_max(1 − e^(−t/τ)), a_max = v_max/τ → two orthogonal traits where `forty` gives one scalar.
- **THREE_CONE_DRILL** ([Texans](https://www.houstontexans.com/news/nfl-combine-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-3-cone-drill)): L-shape, 5 yd per leg, 30 yd total in six segments. Contains **two physically different abilities**: a near-zero-speed 180° brake-and-reverse on the first out-and-back, and a **sustained small-radius arc** on the cone-2/cone-3 figure-8. Record 6.28 s. *The scalar time conflates them — "the time hides two players" is the cleanest figure in the competition.*
- **SHORT_SHUTTLE (5-10-5)**: pure frontal-plane brake-and-reverse, two 180° touch-and-go plants, record 3.81 s. **This is our cut-detector validation set**: known number of known-angle cuts per rep (94% show exactly two ≥150° reversals).
- **GAUNTLET_DRILL** ([CoachesInsider](https://coachesinsider.com/football/wide-receivers-specific-combine-drills/), [CFL manual](https://www.britishamericanfootball.org/downloads/CFL%20Combine%20Drills%20-%20Updated%20Feb%202%202018.pdf)): opening 180° flip between two throwers, then sprint a yard line catching ~6–7 alternating passes from QBs 5–10 yd off the path. Coaching point: **do not gather or slow down for the ball.** The only drill that tests catching at sprint speed. **`entity_type == 'BALL'` timestamps every catch point** → receiver speed at ball arrival is directly computable.
- **RUN_THE_HOOP**: for EDGE/DL this is the **pass-rush arc** — explode from stance, rip/swim past pop-up dummies, bend around a hoop of cones, finish flat to a point 7–10 yd inside the second dummy (the QB's launch point). Inside lean, short choppy steps at the apex, stay tight. **It is the only drill whose geometry literally reproduces a real play's path.** For WR/TE it's curvilinear speed maintenance (corner/post/benders, turning upfield). ([pass-rush hoop drills](http://www.mycoachonline.com/football/pass-rush-hoop-drills))
- **BACK_PEDAL_AND_TRANSITION_45_DEGREE_REACTION / W-drill / pedal-and-flip** ([DB drills](https://coachesinsider.com/football/defensive-backs-specific-combine-drills/)): backpedal ~5 yd on a cue, break forward at 45° to the next cone, repeat, finish on a thrown ball. Observable signal = the **velocity trough at each transition** (speed lost converting backward to forward momentum) and time to recover 90% of forward speed. A **false step is literally visible** as small backward x-displacement after the cue. n = 122 DB, the largest group.
- **PASS_PRO_MIRROR_DRILL**: ~16 s reps of lateral kick-slide staying square to a moving "rabbit." **The rabbit is NOT tracked** — only the prospect wears a sensor, so the stimulus driving every reversal is unobserved. This is why the published attempt failed.
- **OL_PULL_DRILL_FOLD**: flat pull down the LOS around a dummy, turn upfield to a second-level target, then react to a coach's signal and cut back. Unlike the mirror drill the whole path is the athlete's own choice → more tractable; but this dataset's OL endpoints are pass-protection-centric, so it's a weaker bet than it looks.
- **Hardware**: Zebra UWB, same vendor as in-game, two tags per player, **~6-inch location accuracy**, tags blink up to 85 Hz, **downsampled to the 10 Hz we receive**. 2025 was the third year of collection, second year teams had access, first year of a public dashboard. ([Zebra](https://www.zebra.com/us/en/about-zebra/newsroom/press-releases/2023/zebra-technologies-extends-partnership-with-national-football-league.html), [AWS Combine IQ](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/beyond-the-stopwatch-inside-the-nfls-new-interactive-combine-dashboard))

## Methodology notes

**The three bias traps a team-analyst judge spots in under a minute:**

1. **Confounding by draft capital.** Snaps and starts are assigned partly by draft slot and team need, so *"combine trait predicts snaps"* may be entirely *"combine trait predicted draft slot."* **Mandatory:** include `draft_overall_pick` (log-transformed — nfelo's `−4.619·ln(draft_number)` is the live competitor; UDFAs get draft_number = 300 by nfelo convention) plus a UDFA indicator. Report the **nested ladder** explicitly: (1) `ngs_final_score` alone → (2) + `combine_results` box score (`forty`, `ten_yd_split`, `three_cone`, `short_shuttle`, `vertical`, `broad_jump`, `combine_height`, `combine_weight`) → (3) + our drill tracking features. Publish **Δ R² / Δ log-loss / Δ AUC with a confidence interval** for layer 3. If tracking adds nothing over draft slot, say so and pivot to a *within-slot* application.
2. **Exposure asymmetry.** The 2023 class has three seasons of accumulation, 2025 has one. **Any career-total outcome is mechanically larger for older classes.** Use **per-opportunity rates** from `player_play` as the primary DV, and offset/stratify by exposure (snaps, routes, pass-rush snaps) if totals appear at all.
3. **Survivorship / selection.** `player_play` rows exist only for snaps actually played; poor testers are drafted later and play less; UDFAs have null `draft_overall_pick`; the 510-player cohort is **~170/yr against ~320 combine invitees/yr and 770 players drafted 2023–2025** — roughly half the invitees and under a quarter of entrants. **Report who is missing and why** (drill opt-outs are visible as blanks in nflverse `combine.csv`), and consider the **two-stage model**: (a) snaps earned, (b) per-snap quality conditional on playing.

**The leakage trap unique to this dataset:** `ngs_final_score` / `ngs_athleticism_score` / `ngs_college_production_score` are model outputs whose training target (*NFL starter or Pro Bowler within 3 seasons*) is nearly identical to `player_career_successes.csv`. **Using them as features while predicting career success is leakage, not feature engineering.** They are the baseline.

**The second leakage trap:** if you build an "over expected" metric, **keep combine athleticism OUT of the expectation model.** This is the documented Tyreek-Hill-xYAC problem — Hill's speed is baked into the expectation, so he can never be credited for it. Model the expected outcome from **situation only** (down, distance, route, depth, coverage, time after snap, alignment), then regress the **residual** on combine tracking features. `expected_yards_after_catch` is already supplied and situational — verify what it conditions on before trusting it.

**Statistical frame:**
- **Hierarchical / partial pooling is not optional at n≈100 per group.** Per-player estimates need shrinkage toward the position-group mean as a function of attempts/snaps. Borrow nfelo's move: **one explicit, stated shrinkage parameter**, and **plot the shrinkage curve as a figure** — it converts "small sample" from a reviewer objection into a demonstrated design feature.
- **Never pool across positions.** `combine_position` and `nfl_position` are separate columns for a reason; `SKILL_DRILLS_*` are position-specific; the NGS model runs 12 separate position models; SumerScores benchmarks by role *and* alignment. Cross-position pooling inflates correlations through position-average differences — the easiest correctness objection available to a judge.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** train on 2023 + 2024 draft classes, **test on 2025**. (Check first that each drill exists in all three years — `drill_name` values drift, e.g. `LINE` → `LINE_DRILL` — otherwise your held-out year has no data for that drill.)
- **Reliability as a GATING step, not an afterthought.** The `attempt` column is a goldmine almost no submission will use: compute **split-half / Spearman-Brown** for every candidate feature and **drop anything below ~0.5** (the published audit threw out 4 of 13). Where only one attempt exists, use within-season odd/even-snap split-half on the outcome side. **Report the number even if it's 0.2, and benchmark it**: MLB batting average 0.4–0.5; ESPN pass-rush win rate 0.59; ESPN RTM Catch Score 0.38; RYOE 0.21; or the YoY r of the `forty` itself within our own data.
- **Component independence.** If the metric has 2–4 parts, publish the **correlation matrix between components** and argue they are separable skills. This is the direct answer to "isn't this just the 40?"
- **Monotone ladder (free, model-free, and extremely persuasive).** Bucket players by drill-score decile and show the mean NFL outcome moving monotonically. ESPN's 7.2 → −0.7 yards ladder is the template.
- **Leave-one-criterion-out.** Show the result survives dropping the most arbitrary threshold.
- **Pre-commit the feature list at the top of the notebook.** 8 drill types × dozens of kinematic features × 5 position groups × many outcomes = tens of thousands of implicit tests against ~100 players per group. Show the **complete feature ledger including what was dropped**, and use hierarchical shrinkage or explicit FDR. Then *say in the writeup that you pre-committed* — unfalsifiable feature-hunting is exactly what a team analyst suspects.
- **Minimum detectable effect, stated up front.** The published DL audit could only detect r ≳ 0.36 at n=65. Compute our MDE and put it in the writeup — showing you knew your power *before* you looked is the strongest available signal of competence to this panel.
- **Random effects for nuisance structure:** drill reps are not independent (fatigue, rep order within a workout day, coaching-staff and station differences across the three years). Include a year/drill-session random effect.
- **Mandatory controls by endpoint:** DL — exclude `unblocked_pressure` (measures scheme, not rush skill); always weight-adjust get-off (weight+position = adj. R² 0.464). OL — `pass_rushers_encountered` and `blitzing` are mandatory (six-man protection flatters a tackle's `pressure_allowed`). WR/DB — `cushion`, man/zone, cover shell, `route_ran`.
- **Don't slice thin.** Sabin: EPA stability collapses to 0.002 on a narrow situational slice. Aggregate to season/role level.
- **Terminate in a football unit with a stated exchange rate**, written as a one-line equation: *"each 0.1 s faster 45° transition is worth X inches of separation allowed at the catch point, Y EPA per coverage snap, Z points per 600 defensive snaps."* Show a **per-opportunity rate AND a season total** (the DVOA/DYAR pair). Then translate to **tier labels** — Blue-chip movement profile / Starter / Rotational / Developmental.
- **Where uncertainty goes:** error bars and reliability live in the **writeup/methodology**; the player leaderboard table stays clean with point estimates. (nfelo computes per-QB variance internally and publishes point estimates only; ESPN publishes no confidence bands on leaderboards.) *Exception:* with n=510 a leaderboard **figure** without uncertainty bars is noise with logos on it — put intervals on the plot, keep them out of the table.

## Engineering playbook

**Scale reality: this is a ~26–30M-row problem that fits in RAM. Do not build a Dask/Spark story — it costs clarity points.** Measured on BDB-shaped synthetic data: 134 MB CSV → 150 MB RAM naive (f64/i64) → 93 MB with f32/i32 → **62 MB** with f32/i32 + Enum `event` + integer frame index instead of the timestamp string → **25.5 MB as zstd Parquet**. Extrapolating to the real 2.31 GB corpus: **~430–450 MB Parquet, ~1.0–1.2 GB resident, ~85 B/row.**

**Stack decision: Polars-first, DuckDB for exploratory SQL, pandas only at the plotting boundary, pyarrow as interchange.** Measured (polars 2.0.0 vs duckdb 1.5.6, 1.512M rows): ingest 0.31 s vs 2.85 s; group-by 0.11 s vs 0.20 s; rolling-window 0.21 s vs 0.25 s. A notebook that switches tools for no reason reads as undisciplined under the Writeup criterion.

**1. Ingest — write the schema dict by hand, once.** It's 15 lines, it documents your dtype decisions to the judges, and it kills a whole class of silent bugs across the 9 CSVs. `pl.scan_csv(path, schema=SCHEMA)` → `.sink_parquet(..., engine="streaming")`. If types are untrusted, use DuckDB `read_csv(..., sample_size=-1)` — the **default sample_size is 20480 rows**, which is exactly how `event` (mostly empty) and `draft_overall_pick` (null for UDFA) get mis-inferred.

**2. Dtypes.** `nfl_id` → Int32. Derive a compact frame index with `pl.int_range(pl.len()).over("game_id","play_id","nfl_id").cast(pl.Int16)` and **drop the timestamp string** — Int16 covers 32,767 frames (54 min at 10 Hz). *Do not parse the frame out of the ISO timestamp with string slicing* — a tested attempt failed on 5,925/5,925 values because the slice landed on ".90"/".00". Use **Enum** (not Categorical, not String) for `event`, `route_ran`, `coverage_assignment`, `drill_type`, `drill_name`, `nfl_position`, `college_conference` — declaring the domains up front doubles as documentation and catches a typo'd route name at cast time. Note Polars' StringCache is now a no-op (the [categoricals refactor](https://pola.rs/posts/categoricals-refactor/)).

**3. Partition by SEASON (3 values) and/or `nfl_id % 8` — NEVER by `game_id`.** One file per game = ~1,002 tiny files against DuckDB's own *"at least 100 MB per partition"* guidance and its 100-open-file default. With ~450 MB total you want 4–16 files. Since most analyses are per-player across seasons, a **player-hash partition prunes better** than a game partition for the combine-to-performance join. Verified Polars 2.0 idiom: `lf.with_columns(gbucket=(pl.col("game_id") % 8).cast(pl.Int8)).sink_parquet(pl.PartitionBy("pq/", key="gbucket", include_key=True), mkdir=True, compression="zstd", row_group_size=200_000, engine="streaming")`. Read back with `pl.scan_parquet("pq/**/*.parquet", hive_partitioning=True)`.

**4. Compression: zstd.** Measured 25.5 MB vs 36.1 (snappy) vs 38.1 (lz4) on identical data. Write once, read dozens of times → 30% less I/O on every read, for one flag. Set `row_group_size=200_000–250_000` and `statistics=True` so row-group min/max pruning works. **Skip `memory_map=True`** — Arrow docs are explicit that Parquet must be decoded and it *"won't help much with resident memory."* The real wins are projection + predicate pushdown: a single-play read off a 25 MB zstd Parquet took 0.023 s (Polars) / 0.012 s (DuckDB).

**5. Per-play features — Polars expression-level `filter()` inside `agg()`.** Far more readable than SQL `FILTER`/`arg_min`:
```python
.agg(
  x_snap    = pl.col("x").filter(pl.col("event")=="ball_snap").first(),
  s_at_pass = pl.col("s").filter(pl.col("event")=="pass_forward").first(),
  top_s     = pl.col("s").max(),
  dist      = pl.col("dis").sum(),
  peak_decel= pl.col("a").min(),
  net_disp  = ((pl.col("x").last()-pl.col("x").first())**2
             + (pl.col("y").last()-pl.col("y").first())**2).sqrt(),
)  # then straightness = net_disp / dist
```
**6. Within-play windows — `over()` after an explicit `sort()`.** `.sort("game_id","play_id","nfl_id","time").with_columns(vx=(pl.col("x").diff()/0.1).over(k), s_sm=pl.col("s").rolling_mean(window_size=5, center=True, min_samples=1).over(k))`. "Max speed in the next 1 s": `pl.col("s").reverse().rolling_max(10, min_samples=1).reverse().over(k)`. `over()` handles ~90% of windowing with no UDF. **Always sort immediately before** — rolling windows assume ordering.

**7. ⭐ THE BIGGEST WIN: batch all scipy work into ONE call on a padded 2-D array.** Measured **64× faster than `group_by().map_groups()`, with bit-identical output (max abs diff 0.0).** 5,000 series × 70 frames: per-group UDF 2.59 s → batched 0.04 s. 20,000 × 100: 0.06 s.
```python
X  = d["x"].to_numpy().reshape(n_ser, L)
Xb = sosfiltfilt(sos, X, axis=1)
VX = np.gradient(Xb, 0.1, axis=1)
```
For ragged lengths: scatter into a NaN matrix `M[ser_idx, frame_idx] = x`, then **edge-hold the tail** so filtfilt sees a flat tail rather than NaN: `np.where(idx < lens[:,None], M, M[np.arange(n), lens-1][:,None])` (verified 0 NaNs, 0.03 s). This is the difference between a 10-minute feature run and a 2-second one — i.e. between **tuning** your filter and **guessing**. Put it in the writeup; it's the kind of engineering NFL staffers recognize.

**8. ⚠️ NYQUIST: at 10 Hz the Nyquist frequency is 5 Hz, so the textbook biomechanics cutoffs (Winter's 6–7 Hz, the common 10 Hz Butterworth) are mathematically unavailable.** Your usable band is **~1.0–2.5 Hz**. Winter's 6.5–7.0 Hz optimum assumes 100–240 Hz mocap. The validated automated change-of-direction detector for team sport uses a **1.5 Hz** cutoff ([PMC11820490](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820490/)). **Saying "we use a 6 Hz Butterworth, standard in biomechanics" on 10 Hz data is an instant credibility loss with this panel. Saying "Nyquist is 5 Hz, so we swept 1.0–2.5 Hz and report sensitivity" is a credibility gain.**

**9. Filter bake-off (measured, 3 cm positional noise, analytic ground truth, 50° cut at 7 m/s).** Acceleration RMSE in m/s²: **butter2 fc=1.5 → 0.472**; savgol w=7 p=3 → 0.563; butter2 fc=2.0 → 0.583; savgol w=9 → 0.633; butter2 fc=1.0 → 0.715; savgol w=11 → 0.833; butter2 fc=2.5 → 0.837; GCV spline → 1.181; savgol w=5 → 1.228; **raw central differences → 1.354**. Use `sos = butter(2, fc/(fs/2), 'low', output='sos'); xb = sosfiltfilt(sos, X, axis=1)` — `sosfiltfilt` not `filtfilt(b,a)` for numerical stability, and **zero-phase** so cut-event timing isn't shifted.

**10. ⚠️ RMSE hides the metric that matters: PEAK BIAS.** 200-realization Monte Carlo, true peak |a| = 7.63 m/s², true peak κ = 0.370 m⁻¹. Peak |a| bias: **raw central diff +27.1%**; butter2 fc=2.5 +9.1%; **fc=2.0 +3.2%**; savgol w=7 p=3 −2.4%; fc=1.5 −6.0%; savgol w=9 −15.1%; **fc=1.0 −17.8%**; savgol w=11 −22.6%. Peak κ tracks it (fc=2.0 +1.2%, raw +20.2%). **Filter ORDER barely matters (2 vs 4 differ by 0.1%); CUTOFF dominates.** → **Choose by use case and say so: fc≈2.0 Hz (or savgol w=7 p=3) when the feature IS a peak (peak decel, max curvature, min turn radius); fc≈1.5 Hz for clean traces and integrals.** Using one filter for both and not mentioning the bias is the likeliest way a careful judge finds a hole.

**11. Savitzky-Golay entirely inside Polars, no UDF** (verified to match scipy to 2.8e-14): `c = savgol_coeffs(window, poly, deriv=d, delta=0.1, use="dot"); h = window//2; expr = sum(pl.lit(float(c[j])) * pl.col(col).shift(h-j) for j in range(window)).over(parts)`. Edges come back null by design (scipy's `mode='interp'` fits an edge polynomial instead) — drop the first/last `h` frames. Streaming-safe and parallelizes across plays for free.

**12. ⚠️ `filtfilt` silently fails on short series.** `padlen = 3·max(len(a),len(b)) = 3·(2·order+1)` → **order 2 needs ≥16 frames (1.6 s), order 4 needs ≥28 frames (2.8 s).** Many BDB play-player segments are shorter. **Add an `n_frames` guard and a `filter_used` column** routing short series to savgol w=5/7. Silently dropping short plays biases the sample toward long developing plays — an easy, fatal bias for any claim about explosiveness.

**13. Curvature, guarded.** `kappa = |vx·ay − vy·ax| / max(hypot(vx,vy), v_min)**3` with **v_min ≈ 1.5–2.0 m/s**, and *mask frames below that speed entirely*. Without the guard your "sharpest cutters" leaderboard becomes a list of players standing still. Report the interpretable inverse, **turn radius R = 1/κ** (recovered R_min = 2.70 m exactly on the simulated cut). **Better still, decompose:** `a_tan = (vx·ax + vy·ay)/|v|` (braking/driving) and `a_rad = (vx·ay − vy·ax)/|v|` (signed centripetal, left/right) — these cleanly separated into −6.26 m/s² braking and +6.32 m/s² centripetal at the cut. **a_tan / a_rad is the decomposition a coach understands**: "he can brake AND turn" vs "he has to slow down to turn." Maps directly onto 3-cone and shuttle.

**14. The complete bridge-metric recipe (verified end-to-end against analytic ground truth).** Filter position (butter2, fc 1.5–2.0, sosfiltfilt) → vx, vy → `heading = np.unwrap(np.arctan2(vy,vx))` → `omega = np.gradient(heading, dt)`. Detect the cut as `find_peaks(-speed, prominence=0.5)` co-located with `find_peaks(np.abs(omega), prominence=0.5)` (both fired on frame 19 against a true cut at frame 20). Window = contiguous frames where `|omega| > 0.2·max|omega|`. **Seven numbers out:** entry speed (5.66 m/s), exit speed (5.76), speed lost (1.53), speed retention ratio (1.02), time-in-cut (0.7 s), peak |omega| (1.48 rad/s = 85°/s), peak a_tan (−6.26 m/s²), R_min (2.70 m); heading change read 40.9° vs a true 50° — **the ~18% under-read is the filter, so report it**. Scale-free shape features: straightness = net_disp/path_length (0.917), total |Δheading| (56.2°), ∫κ ds (0.967 rad). **The same seven numbers run on THREE_CONE / SHORT_SHUTTLE / BACK_PEDAL_AND_TRANSITION *and* on in-game route breaks and pursuit angles — same units, same code path. That identical code path IS the "non-obvious, actionable linkage."**

**15. ⚠️ Do not trust vendor `s` and `a` as ground truth, and never mix them with derived values in the same feature.** NGS reports location/speed/distance/acceleration at 10 Hz from Zebra, but the smoothing is proprietary and undocumented. **Run and show three cheap validations:** (a) does `cumsum(dis)` track path length from successive (x,y)? **20. Field rendering — hand-roll from NFL Rule 1 constants with `LineCollection`.** ~40 lines, <1 s, no dependency risk. 120 × 53.333 yd (BDB coords map 1:1); **inbound hash marks 70'9" = 23.583 yd from each sideline**; hash marks 4 in × 24 in; **yard numbers' bottom edge exactly 12 yd from the sideline**; all lines 4 in wide except goal line and yellow lines at 8 in ([rulebook](https://operations.nfl.com/the-rules/nfl-rulebook/), [2026 rules PDF](https://media.nfl.com/content/dam/communications/Official%20Playing%20Rules/2026%20Official%20Playing%20Rules.pdf)). Batch every repeated mark into **one** `LineCollection` (99 ticks × 4 positions = 396 artists otherwise). Library alternatives: [sportypy](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sportypy) (`NFLField` with `display_range` zoom), [nfl-tracks](https://pypi.org/project/nfl-tracks/). **Wrong hash-mark placement is the tell that you used a generic template, and this panel watches football every day.**

**21. Animation — matplotlib `FuncAnimation` with artist mutation + `to_jshtml()`.** Pre-create artists, then in `update(frame)` call `scat.set_offsets(np.stack([x,y]).T)`, `line.set_data(...)`, `txt.set_text(...)` and return the tuple ([docs](https://matplotlib.org/stable/users/explain/animations/animations.html)). **Avoid Plotly animations above ~200 frames** — performance degrades and Scattergl has a known bug where frames after the first don't render. A 7-second play is 70 frames (fine); a drill montage is not. **Budget exactly one animated figure** against the <10 cap, and make it the side-by-side combine-drill-vs-in-game-rep comparison.

## Pitfalls to avoid

**Fatal / mechanical**
1. **Notebook left private, writeup left in draft at the deadline, no media-gallery cover image, >2,000 words, ≥10 figures, or a notebook that doesn't touch the tracking data (auto-unscored).** Put a compliance block in the pre-submit ritual: word count from the *rendered* markdown, figures+tables ≤9, notebook public and re-run top-to-bottom, cover image uploaded, **writeup actually SUBMITTED**, correct track chosen.
2. **Notebook throws on the judges' re-run.** Polars 2.0 shipped Oct 6 2026 (`pl.PartitionByKey` gone; Parquet ENUM reads back as String); Kaggle is now Python 3.13 / 4 cores / 30 GB / 12 h. Pin versions and test on Kaggle, not just locally.

**Statistical / correctness**
3. **No draft-position control.** If the effect disappears once `draft_overall_pick` is in the model, we have rediscovered the draft.
4. **Career-total outcomes across unequal exposure** (2023 class has 3 seasons, 2025 has 1). Use per-opportunity rates.
5. **Feeding `ngs_*` scores in as predictors and then claiming novelty.** Their target *is* our outcome. They are the baseline.
6. **Building an "over expected" metric with combine athleticism inside the expectation** — this mechanically strips the signal you're measuring (the Tyreek-Hill-xYAC problem).
7. **Pooling across positions.** Inflates correlations through position-average differences; the easiest objection to raise.
8. **Silent multiple comparisons.** 8 drills × dozens of features × 5 groups × many outcomes vs ~100 players per group.
9. **Slicing thin.** EPA stability collapses to 0.002 on a narrow situational slice (vs 0.093 for plain yards).
10. **Over-modeling tiny n.** A deep net at n≈100 reads as naive; a past finalist used **no ML at all**.
11. **No reliability reported.** Or worse, omitting it *because it's low*. The convention is publish-and-benchmark.
12. **Trusting vendor `s`/`a` as ground truth**, or mixing them with derived values in one feature. And **`dis` is unreliable** (~37 yd over a 40-yd dash).

**Signal processing**
13. **NYQUIST TRAP:** citing a 6–10 Hz Butterworth on 10 Hz data. Nyquist is 5 Hz. Usable band ~1.0–2.5 Hz.
14. **PEAK BIAS:** raw central differences overstate peak |a| by **+27%** and peak κ by **+20%**; fc=1.0 Hz understates by **−18%**. Use fc≈2.0 (or savgol w=7 p=3) for peak features, fc≈1.5 for traces, and show the sensitivity.
15. **CURVATURE SINGULARITY:** without a v_min guard (~1.5–2.0 m/s), "sharpest cutters" becomes "players standing still."
16. **`filtfilt` on short series:** order 2 needs ≥16 frames, order 4 needs ≥28. Silently dropping short plays biases toward long developing plays.
17. **Per-group scipy UDFs:** 64× slower than the padded 2-D batch for identical output — the difference between tuning your filter and guessing.
18. **Treating combine tracking like game tracking:** no `o` column, per-drill coordinate frames, multiple attempts, drifting `drill_name`s, erroneous rows. **Unnormalized traces measure the drill setup, not the athlete.**
19. **Over-partitioning** (1,002 one-file-per-game Parquets) and **memory_map theatre** (doesn't reduce resident memory for Parquet).

**Domain / framing**
20. **Claiming the 40 predicts get-off.** It is self-started with a hand start — **zero reaction time**.
21. **Any "new" speed feature.** Tracked peak speed ≈ the official 40 (r ≈ −0.98). Residualize against `combine_results` or it's the 40 in disguise. Never present a tracked time as "the 40" (~0.3 s slow).
22. **Re-running the two published negative results unchanged** (mirror-drill → OL pressure; DL speed features → get-off).
23. **Forgetting the other 21 players.** In-game tracking covers ONLY the 510 cohort. You **cannot** compute separation, cushion, or block-win geometry yourself; you must use the charted `player_play` fields and **say so explicitly in the body text**, the way ESPN states *"the system knows neither the blocker nor defender assignments."* The ghost/space-control device that won repeatedly **cannot be rebuilt here** — make that asymmetry the submission's structural idea, not an obstacle.
24. **For DL endpoints:** `unblocked_pressure` measures scheme, not rush skill — exclude it. **For OL:** `pass_rushers_encountered` and `blitzing` are mandatory (six-man protection flatters a tackle's rate). **Always weight-adjust get-off.**
25. **Reps are not independent:** fatigue, rep order within a workout day, station/staff differences across three years. Include a year/session random effect.

**Writing / presentation**
26. **Scope creep into "a combine athleticism score for all 510 players."**
27. **Buried lede.** Method-first loses a reader grading at 11 pm after a day job. Answer in the first 60 words.
28. **Building a draft-value or bust-probability predictor.** Every team has one; nothing in the eight-year canon has ever won that way; it fails the week-to-week usability line.
29. **Illegible figures, default palettes, tiny legends.**
30. **Dashboard-centric submission.** It counts as one figure and judges are told not to explore it.
31. **Inventing jargon.** The league's own lexicon is already top speed / acceleration / change of direction / 10-yard split / size-speed composite. Use NGS's thresholds (2.5 s, 75%) rather than inventing cutoffs.
32. **Hiding every constant inside a fitted model.** The credible precedent is a named threshold in a visible config block with a plain-English justification for each (ESPN's 2.5 s; nfelo's 25-Elo-per-point and 3.3 multiplier in one `model_config.json`).
33. **Wrong football vocabulary or mislabeled positions/techniques.** Instant credibility loss. Have a football-literate person read the draft and a non-football person read for clarity.
34. **Overclaiming.** 510 players, 1–3 seasons, one cohort, rookies only. **Write a named "What this metric does not do" section** — nfelo's credibility comes substantially from publicly arguing against its own metric.
35. **Ignoring prior BDB work.** Lopez values submissions that build on one another; citing 1–2 past entries we extend is cheap credibility.
36. **Spending the last week polishing the model instead of the prose.** The most-cited unforced error, from both winners and the NFL's own grader.
37. **Caching:** several publishers (wideleft.football, grindingthemocks.com, GitHub release downloads under sustained use) intermittently refuse connections. **Cache every external file locally once and commit the cache; never let the pipeline re-fetch at runtime.** And decide early whether the notebook must run with **internet disabled** — if so, every external join must be baked into an attached Kaggle Dataset, which changes the whole pipeline shape.
