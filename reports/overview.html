NFL Big Data Bowl 2027  ·  Project brief  ·  10 Oct 2026

Combine to Kickoff

The NFL has released every frame of Combine sensor tracking that exists — three draft classes, 2023 to 2025 — alongside in-game tracking for the same 510 players. The task is to find a link between how a prospect moves in Indianapolis in February and how he plays on Sundays. This is a judged hackathon: no leaderboard, no metric, no submission file. Just 2,000 words, nine figures, and a panel of NFL team analysts.

Three facts that change the analysis

Trap

2024 back-pedal reps are truncated

They stop before the break: median rep 6.0 s versus 11.6 s in 2023, with the braking phase simply absent. Pool the years and rep-to-rep reliability looks like r = 0.80; within a year it is ≈ 0.

Trap

2025 is a different export

2023–24 coordinates sit on a 1/60-yard grid; 2025 does not, and its median peak speed runs lower. Every drill metric has to be standardised within (drill, draft_year) — fitted on training folds only.

Opening

13 players never took a snap

The host flagged this as analytically interesting rather than as dirt: perhaps their Combine performance is why they never got the opportunity. A free, host-endorsed hook for the selection story.

Measurement caution

Tracked 40-yard times run about 0.3 s slower than the official electronic clock, and dis under-counts distance — summing to roughly 37 yards over a 40-yard dash. Neither is a bug to fix; both are reasons never to present a tracking-derived time as "the 40", and to integrate path length from the smoothed trajectory instead.

02

What has to be modelled

Every judge is an NFL team analyst who already believes the Combine predicts nothing — the published base rate is around 2.6% of performance variance. So the finding cannot be a correlation. It has to survive draft position.

The method chain

1 · Features from raw trajectory

Not from the shipped s/a, which carry an undocumented filter. Re-derive with an explicit 2 Hz zero-phase Butterworth, split acceleration into braking and turning, detect cuts, fit a sprint profile.

2 · Reliability before validity

Most drills have two reps — a ready-made test-retest design. A metric's correlation with anything is capped at √reliability, so the ceiling gets reported next to every result.

3 · Orthogonalise

Residualise against the official drill time and body mass. What survives is the part of the movement signature the stopwatch cannot see — which is the only part worth a submission.

4 · Adjust and shrink

Incremental R² over a draft-capital baseline. Partial pooling so a 40-snap rookie does not outrank a starter on a noisy rate.

5 · Leave one class out

Three draft classes is the whole sample. Train on 2023–24, test on 2025 — and fit every transform inside the fold, or the evaluation quietly becomes transductive.

6 · State the power first

Compute the minimum detectable effect before looking. At n≈70 that is around r = 0.36. Showing you knew is the cheapest credibility available.

A structural edge worth naming

The incumbent — the Next Gen Stats draft model — is a position-specific gradient boosting model with monotonic constraints. A monotone model cannot represent an optimum; it has no way to encode "too much of this is bad". Any genuine inverted-U in the tracking data is therefore something the incumbent is structurally incapable of finding, not merely something it happened to miss.

03

Where the signal probably is

Tracked peak speed is essentially the 40 time restated. All the upside is in shape-of-motion inside the position drills. Effective samples are 40–120 players per group, so the plan is one lead question, one supporting result, and nothing else.

#QuestionGroupCombine signalNFL outcomenRisk
1 Transition tax → deployment. Does the cost of a pedal-to-break transition predict the cushion his own coaching staff gives him? DB back-pedal & 45° reaction: velocity-trough depth, recovery time, L/R asymmetry cushion, then separation allowed in man ~90 low
2 Speed retention through the catch → YAC over expected. A receiver who gathers for the ball bleeds the momentum that makes yards after it. WR·TE gauntlet: speed at ball arrival ÷ speed 0.5 s earlier, plus consistency yards_after_catch − expected ~95 medium
3 Brake versus arc, route-conditional. Braking should predict separation on sharp breaks, bend on speed routes — and nothing should predict the average. WR 3-cone & shuttle plants vs. hoop arc separation_at_pass_forward by route family ~90 medium
4 Bend quality on the arc → time to pressure. The drill geometry is the speed-rush path. EDGE apex speed ÷ straight-line peak; min turn radius time_to_pressure 40–50 high
5 Left/right asymmetry as a scouting number — scouts have always said "stiff turning one way". WR·DB left vs right slot routes; the athlete is his own control separation by route direction ~90 low
6 Motion-onset latency from cued drills → get-off. Directly answers a published null. DL cue-to-movement time in bag drills player_get_off 65–68 very high
— Known dead end: pass-pro mirror reversals → pressure allowed. Published negative result; revisit only by narrowing the endpoint, or by making the failure itself the point. OL PASS_PRO_MIRROR_DRILL pressure_allowed 57 avoid

Sample sizes are drawn from other participants' public exploration and are unverified. The first job once the data lands is to re-derive every one of them.

That 40% is the asymmetry worth exploiting. It is almost entirely under our control, and it is where a field of Kaggle-native entrants reliably underinvests. Nine figure slots

  • Hero. One player's trace against the cohort envelope, annotated in words on the chart.
  • The draft-slot-adjusted effect. The figure that answers the only real objection.
  • Validation panel. Split-half reliability, held-out class, calibration.
  • ⭐ The differentiator. The same athlete's Combine drill trace beside his NFL in-game trace — same units, same code path. No prior Big Data Bowl could produce this image.
  • Leaderboard. Real names, uncertainty bars, team marks.
  • Monotone ladder. Decile of drill score against mean outcome. No model required, and among the most persuasive charts available.
  • The "tells" table. Condition, observed rate, predicted rate, how often it occurs — against a cohort base rate.
  • Application mock-up. A draft card with the metric sitting next to the 40 and the NGS score.
  • Reserve. Filter sensitivity, or the vendor-vs-derived validation panel.
Mechanics that zero a submission

Notebook left private · writeup left in draft at the deadline · no Media Gallery cover image · over 2,000 words · ten or more figures · a notebook that never touches the tracking data. Each of these is an automatic loss, independent of how good the work is.

05

Where the build stands

Shipped

  • Competition, rules and rubric captured; every host clarification on the forum read and recorded
  • All 9 files and 158 columns documented, annotated where the shipped data contradicts the published dictionary
  • Signal-processing core: explicit filtering, braking/turning split, curvature, cut detection, sprint profiling — validated against synthetic ground truth
  • Per-attempt Combine features, in-game outcome aggregation, reliability and draft-capital controls
  • An audit script that re-derives every data claim from the shipped files rather than trusting the forum
  • Synthetic fixtures reproducing the real schema and its traps, so the pipeline runs end to end before the download

Blocked or open

  • No competition data. Needs a Kaggle account that has joined the hackathon, plus an API token. Everything downstream waits on this.
  • GitHub push is unauthenticated on this machine. Commits are safe locally.
  • Open or University track? Both are $45,000; University is restricted to entirely-undergraduate teams and is a far weaker field.
  • Which question? Recommendation is the DB transition tax, with gauntlet speed retention as the headline figure if its ball-tracking coverage survives the audit.
  • A football voice. One quoted sentence from someone who has coached the position is disproportionately persuasive to this panel.
